# Nationwide product review — LTV Desking PRO

**Review date:** 2026-10-08. **Baseline:** local `codex/deal-sheet-polish`, HEAD `007134a`; this report accompanies subsequent local changes and does not assert deployment. **Decision:** continue a bounded Michigan retail-finance companion pilot; do not sell this as a nationwide tax engine, lender-decision platform or DMS replacement yet.

This is an engineering/product readiness review for an acquiring company, integration partner or dealer principal. Repository behavior, reproducible synthetic cases and official vendor/regulatory sources control the conclusions. Competitor documentation establishes advertised capabilities, not independently measured reliability or superiority. No real customer/credit records were collected, no lender application was submitted, and no national legal certification is claimed.

## Integrated implementation status

The [launch handoff](LAUNCH_REVIEW_2026-10-08.md) records final verification and supersedes the baseline observations below where fixes were integrated. Imported/edited programs now require human source review, source/version and review time are stored, supplied expiration dates are enforced, and new/used/certified condition is scoped and persisted per VIN. Desk/worksheet labels now identify nominal interest rate. Source authenticity, immutable document/reviewer history, authoritative book provenance, funded reconciliation and national jurisdiction rules are still open. The baseline findings remain here to explain the acceptance criteria rather than to claim those completed repairs are absent.

## Product positioning and competitive evidence

The practical opportunity is an explainable, whole-inventory structuring companion: fast imported inventory, extracted dealer-supplied lender rules, missing-input holds, payment/budget/gross tradeoffs and a resumable internal deal worksheet. This can reduce manual spreadsheet work when tested in a real dealer workflow. Whole-inventory matching by itself is not unique: DealerCenter publishes an I-MAXX inventory-approval workflow. Paid differentiation must therefore be demonstrated through accurate rules, transparent evidence, ease of setup and measured dealer time savings.

## Meaningful ratings: what the current numbers establish

The new `rules-v1` assessment is preferable to the legacy approval-odds heuristic because every displayed ratio has a reproducible numerator and denominator. It does not need imaginary approval data or opaque weights. The current definitions are in `docs/desking/deal-ratings.md` and `services/dealAssessment.ts:170`.

- **Deal readiness:** checks passed / twelve. Missing and failed checks earn no points. It combines data entry, customer budget and dealer gross checks; it is not a pure creditworthiness or customer-benefit rating.
- **Data complete:** resolved checks / twelve, including failures. Completeness and feasibility remain distinct.
- **Lender match:** fully checked fitting programs / fully checked programs; display sample/pending exclusions and denominator. One fit out of one configured lender is not broad market coverage.
- **Budget:** payment / entered ceiling and dollar headroom. PTI/DTI are descriptive ratios unless a configured lender rule supplies a threshold.
- **Gross:** entered all-in VIN cost, product cost and actual expected reserve/flat. Imported acquisition cost and interest-rate spread do not establish actual profit.
- **Tradeoffs:** only fully assessed feasible structures enter Pareto comparison. Lower payment/interest and higher dealer gross do not measure suitability, vehicle quality or customer preferences.

A twelve-of-twelve score means those configured checks passed. It does not establish tax correctness, source authenticity, required documents, regulatory disclosures, lender approval or funding. The old model card's approval-odds description was stale relative to the implemented checklist; this pass rewrites it around actual behavior and compatibility fields. No independently calibrated approval probabilities or observed adoption/funding dataset was available, so no fabricated star rating, approval percentage or company-wide accuracy score is reported.

## Five product gaps found at baseline

### 1. National jurisdiction/transaction coverage — launch-critical for national accuracy claims

**Observed:** `services/calculator.ts:128-144` explicitly models a Michigan dealer; `types.ts:296` contains only MI/OH/IN/IL/FL; `Settings` has no distinct dealer jurisdiction or delivery-location input. A single custom rate changes percentage, not the entire jurisdiction's taxable-base, trade, exemption, product, local tax or fee rules. It must not be marketed as national support.

**Authoritative boundary:** [2026 Michigan Treasury Form 485](https://www.michigan.gov/taxes/-/media/Project/Websites/taxes/Forms/SUW/TY2026/485_11-25.pdf) confirms the $12,000 motor-vehicle trade allowance and lesser-of reciprocal calculation. It also distinguishes delivery outside Michigan, exempt/nonreciprocal states, special registration and possible home-state/local liabilities. These transaction facts are not all modeled. The form contains an apparent example-A discrepancy ($700 arithmetic vs $750 prose); use the controlling rule/verified calculation rather than copying that example's final prose.

**Bounded next step:** explicit dealer-state/onboarding coverage gate and visible buyer-state labels; unsupported transaction estimate unavailable until verified tax/fee inputs are supplied under a defined manual workflow. A production national engine requires official state/local rules or a contracted provider, effective-date versioning and tax breakdown persistence. Do not implement fifty invented rates.

**Acceptance:** unsupported dealer/delivery/state combinations cannot silently use MI rules; hand-verified authoritative cases cover MI trade cap, supported reciprocal buyers, out-of-state delivery and unknown local liabilities; quote/output records show source, rule version and any manual override.

### 2. Program provenance, review and expiration — launch-critical for trusted lender-fit claims

**Observed:** `LenderProfile` has `effectiveDate`, descriptive enrichment URLs and a sample flag, but no authoritative document/version, explicit expiry or reviewer history (`types.ts:210-236`). Replacing extracted terms clears `isSample` automatically in `components/AiLenderManagerModal.tsx:383-388`. Non-sample plus enforceable constraints is currently called a verified match without proving authoritative verification. Range holds guard implausible values, not plausible transcription errors or missing clauses.

**Bounded next step:** require human review of extracted financial terms against the supplied lender sheet; record source document/version, verified-by/time and explicit expiration when supplied. Unknown expiry stays unknown; past effective date alone does not imply stale. Review/expired/unverified programs remain Pending and are excluded from verified-match claims. Show side-by-side source/evidence instead of treating an AI confidence number as certification.

The rate-floor correction requires a known published rate. A coordinated server signal, `rateCheckRequired: true`, keeps redacted sales tiers Pending for manager review rather than silently bypassing the private rate constraint. Managers evaluate the actual published floor. No private rate value is exposed through that signal; final server-hook tests belong to the integrating security review.

**Acceptance:** realistic but incorrectly transcribed rate/LTV cannot gain a verified path through extraction alone; reviewer identity/version persists; expired program never counts as Fit; superseded documents cannot silently overwrite the terms used on a historical quote. Malformed profile/empty program must not become a fully checked lender denominator entry solely by returning an error.

### 3. Per-VIN condition and book/advance basis — launch-critical for mixed-lot/lender-specific accuracy

**Observed:** `Vehicle` stores two nominal book amounts but no guide edition/date/options/source or explicit per-VIN condition (`types.ts:1-16`). Condition lives on `DealData` and the one scenario is applied to every unit (`hooks/useProcessedInventory.ts:98-103`). Mixed new/used inventory can therefore be evaluated under one selected condition. Lender books intentionally do not cross-fallback (`services/bookValue.ts:25-28`); `maxAdvance` and certified status stay pending because their needed semantics are unavailable.

**Bounded next step:** persist per-VIN new/used/certified status with manual/import source, and lender-requested guide/basis/date/options evidence. Define each advance numerator and permitted products/fees from actual lender documents. Do not infer new/used from year or invent universal max-advance formulas. Correct evidence labels so Trade-only lender matching is never described as Retail fallback.

**Acceptance:** two same-year units with different confirmed conditions follow different program rules; missing required book remains Pending; Trade and Retail lenders use their own confirmed values; stale/unknown book provenance is visible; lender-specific advance fixtures reconcile with the source sheet and cash-down/product treatment.

### 4. Connected deal lifecycle and funded reconciliation — launch-critical for enterprise workflow evidence, staged integration roadmap

**Observed:** examined saved-deal interfaces contain customer/salesperson names, vehicle/structure snapshots and entered inputs (`types.ts:251-269`); pipeline statuses are manually changed (`components/screens/PipelineScreen.tsx:187-217`). A status label does not prove a lender decision. Descriptive stipulations and a portal URL do not prove completion, document acceptance or funding. The pilot explicitly treats the DMS as the source for funded payments (`docs/PILOT_CHARTER.md:25-30`).

**Bounded next step:** stable customer/deal IDs, revision-aware save/reopen, owner/manager handoff, selected program/version, decision source/date and a separate verified funded outcome with contract payment/principal/rate/term. Begin with a human reconciliation workflow and authorized export; add RouteOne/Dealertrack/DMS integration only through supported partner interfaces.

**Acceptance:** one customer can hold distinct quoted options without duplicate ambiguity; simultaneous edits cannot silently lose changes; sales/manager handoff preserves private fields; approved and funded states require clearly identified human/provider evidence; five independent-dealer funded deals can be matched back to quote revisions with explained deltas. Customer messaging/submission is separately authorized, never automatic from this audit.

### 5. Contract-rate/payment assumptions and unsupported products — launch-critical for exact payment claims; broader structures are roadmap

**Observed:** `calculateMonthlyPayment` uses equal-monthly amortization (`services/calculator.ts:78-99`); entered `interestRate` drives payments. At baseline, UI labels included APR; the integrated changes now label the nominal interest rate and retain the disclosure-APR limitation. First-payment timing, daily accrual and lender fee/finance-charge classification are absent. The fixed twelve-check assessment does not validate those facts. The finance reserve tool is an interest-spread estimate, not a lender's actual reserve settlement; the gross assessment appropriately requires explicit reserve input.

**Authority:** [CFPB interest rate vs APR](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/ask-cfpb/what-is-the-difference-between-a-loan-interest-rate-and-the-apr-en-733/) distinguishes nominal interest from APR including loan fees and requires comparison of equivalent rates. This supports precise estimate labels; it does not certify this tool's disclosures. [Michigan DIFS documentary-fee bulletin](https://www.michigan.gov/difs/-/media/Project/Websites/difs/Bulletins/2025/Bulletin_2025-03-CF.pdf?rev=8ebf18d6269147b6a80ec6a5bc37c345) supplies a statutory ceiling subject to its terms, not a universal fee allowance.

**Bounded next step:** clearly identify entered contract/nominal rate, monthly-payment assumption and preliminary internal estimate; capture actual funded terms for reconciliation. Add lender-supported first-payment/fee conventions only with source-backed tests. Cash, lease, balloon, BHPH, business contracts, co-applicants, incentive compatibility and full disclosure/contracting require explicit separate workflows; do not force them through the retail-finance twelve-check rubric.

**Acceptance:** the payment shown is tied to recorded rate type/term/principal; unknown lender timing/finance fees are disclosed as unverified assumptions; funded reconciliations distinguish input changes from computation defects; consumer-facing outputs are not portrayed as compliant contracts merely because an internal disclaimer exists.

## Evidence pilot and commercialization gates

The existing pilot charter calls for a Michigan DMS companion, at least three weekly desk users, ten deals/week and weekly funded-payment reconciliation. These are prospective targets. Independently verify the other store's data rather than blending founder-store dogfood with the evidence pilot.

Capture a stable quote/revision and program/tax/book versions, start/end timestamps for desk work, entered-vs-funded principal/rate/term/payment, mismatch cause and denominator. Useful measurable outcomes are: median desk-to-handoff time; proportion of quotes with all source-required facts; unexplained payment delta rate; reviewed-program freshness coverage; funded quote-to-contract match count; and workflow abandonment. Report counts and uncertainty with each percentage. The pilot's ≥80% within $10/month is a learning target; unexplained errors should be corrected even when that target is met.

A national release decision requires product jurisdiction coverage, lender/program evidence and funded workflow results in addition to the separate security/operations gates. The review does not establish customer demand, willingness to pay, product-market fit, national legal compliance or competitor replacement value. Those require external dealer evidence and supported integration contracts.
